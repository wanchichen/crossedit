Abstract

Multi-media editing requires generative models to interpret complex, compositional instructions and modify only the desired elements across modalities - a capability that remains largely beyond existing systems. Current approaches are typically limited to simple, single-attribute edits within a single modality, and scaling to richer instructions is bottlenecked by the difficulty of obtaining diverse, high-quality editing pairs, particularly for cross-modal tasks where aligned audiovisual training data is scarce. We present CrossEdit, a unified omni-modal editing model for images, audio, and video that overcomes this limitation through cross-modal transfer. Our key observation is that complex instruction-following, once learned in any modality, can generalize to others. We exploit the additive nature of acoustic signals to procedurally generate large-scale audio editing pairs with compositional instructions, and show that fine-tuning on this synthetic data — alongside a targeted set of curated cross-modal tasks — induces instruction-following capabilities that transfer zero-shot to unseen modality and task compositions. CrossEdit enables entirely new cross-modal capabilities unsupported by existing models, including audio-visual speech editing and audio-visual scene editing, while retaining strong performance on standard image, video, and audio editing benchmarks. We encourage readers to explore CrossEdit's full capabilities here on our demo website.
